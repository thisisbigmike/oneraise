# OneRaise Pitch Deck

## Core Thesis

**OneRaise is a borderless crowdfunding platform with OneRaise Protect built in: an escrow, verification, milestone-release, and refund layer for campaigns where trust matters.**

The simple positioning:

**OneRaise helps people raise globally. OneRaise Protect makes sure funds move with proof.**

This combined deck should pitch both:
- the broad platform: campaign pages, global donors, multi-rail payments, creator dashboards
- the Protect layer: escrow, proof, verification, refunds, milestone payouts, grant distribution

Typography:
- Headline: Bricolage Grotesque, Sora, or Space Grotesk.
- Body: Instrument Sans, Inter, or Manrope.
- Data labels: Inter Mono or IBM Plex Mono.

Design rule:
Use dark slides for trust, money movement, and Protect. Use light mint slides for personas, go-to-market, and business model. Show product UI or product-like escrow consoles wherever possible.

Full-screen dark slide with a globe, campaign cards, payment rails, and a Protect escrow badge.

Speaker note:
Start with the full platform story, then immediately introduce Protect as the reason OneRaise is more than another donation page.

## Slide 2: The Problem

Title:
**Crowdfunding moves money, but often fails to move trust.**

Main copy:
- Backers often lose visibility once they donate.
- Creators in emerging markets can be blocked by payment rails, geography, and platform requirements.
- Emergency aid and medical campaigns need speed without sacrificing verification.
- NGOs, DAOs, and grant programs need milestone payouts and proof-of-work before release.
- Most platforms separate fundraising, verification, payout, refund handling, and reporting.

Problem line:
**Money can cross borders faster than trust can.**

Suggested visual:
Three-column problem slide:
- Backers: "Where did my money go?"
- Creators: "How do I receive global support?"
- Programs: "How do we release funds responsibly?"

Speaker note:
Keep this direct. The pain is not only fundraising. The pain is confidence after the money leaves the donor.

## Slide 3: Why Now

Title:
**The rails are ready. The accountability layer is missing.**

Main copy:
- Mobile money and digital accounts are expanding access to financial services across emerging markets.
- Stablecoin and crypto settlement rails are making global transfer faster and more programmable.
- Cross-border giving still suffers from cost, friction, compliance complexity, and weak transparency.
- Donors increasingly expect proof, receipts, updates, and visible outcomes.
- The next crowdfunding platform will not only collect funds. It will verify how funds are released.

Suggested visual:
Market timing triangle:
- Digital payment adoption
- Stablecoin settlement
- Demand for verified impact

Speaker note:
This slide should explain why OneRaise can exist now: rails are improving, but trust workflows are still underserved.

## Slide 4: The Solution

Title:
**OneRaise combines global fundraising with protected fund release.**

Main copy:
- Creators launch campaign pages with story, goal, category, image, and payout preference.
- Backers donate by card, local transfer, crypto deposit, or Jupiter-routed Solana tokens.
- Standard campaigns can collect and track donations globally.
- Protected campaigns hold funds in escrow until progress is verified.
- Milestones, documents, receipts, photos, reports, or verifier approvals unlock payouts.
- Failed or unverified campaigns can keep funds refundable according to campaign rules.

Product loop:
**Create -> Collect -> Verify -> Release -> Report**

Suggested visual:
Product flow with two paths:
- Standard: Create -> Collect -> Payout
- Protect: Create -> Collect -> Escrow -> Verify -> Release or Refund

Speaker note:
Make the split clear. OneRaise supports normal crowdfunding, but Protect is the wedge for high-trust campaigns.

Title:

Mode 1: Standard Crowdfunding
- Creators raise from global supporters with campaign pages, donor records, and payout tracking.

Mode 2: Protected Crowdfunding

Mode 3: Emergency Aid Escrow

Suggested visual:

Speaker note:
This shows that Protect is not a single feature. It is an escrow engine that can support several money workflows.

## Slide 6: OneRaise Protect

Title:
**Protect turns fundraising into a verified funding lifecycle.**

Lifecycle:
- **Deposit:** backers or sponsors fund escrow through supported rails or stablecoins.
- **Lock:** funds are reserved against campaign rules, thresholds, or verification requirements.
- **Verify:** creators submit proof such as documents, receipts, photos, reports, vendor details, or proof-of-work.
- **Release:** approved funds unlock to creators, vendors, or grantees.
- **Refund:** failed campaigns keep funds refundable or redirectable according to campaign rules.

Status language:
**Funding -> Locked -> Pending Verification -> Released or Refunded**

Suggested visual:
Escrow console with four balances:
- Total Protected
- Refundable
- Pending Review
- Released

Speaker note:
This is the core Protect slide. Judges, partners, and investors should understand the fund lifecycle at a glance.

## Slide 8: How It Works for Creators and Programs

Title:
**Credible creators unlock more trust. Programs get payout control.**

For creators:
- Set campaign goal, category, story, payout preference, and Protect settings.
- Submit milestone proof when work progresses.
- Unlock tranches instead of asking backers for blind trust.
- Track donations, balances, payout methods, and release status.

For programs:
- Fund escrow once.
- Assign milestones or proof-of-work requirements.
- Review evidence before payout.
- Track grantees, releases, refunds, and payment records in one place.

Suggested visual:
Split slide:
- Creator dashboard: milestone proof submission
- Program dashboard: approve release button

Speaker note:
Protect should not feel anti-creator. It helps trustworthy creators and programs raise more because backers understand the rules.

Positioning line:
**OneRaise separates donor choice from creator payout complexity.**

Suggested visual:
Card / local transfer / crypto / Solana tokens -> OneRaise ledger -> creator / vendor / grantee.

Speaker note:
Avoid turning this into an integration list. The value is that donors and recipients can use different rails while OneRaise preserves the trust record.

## Slide 13: Business Model

Title:
**Revenue grows from transactions and trust workflows.**

Confirmed from current product messaging:
- First campaign: `0%` platform fee.
- Later campaigns: `5%` platform fee.
- Standard payment processing fees still apply.

Recommended Protect revenue layers:
- Protect workflow fee for escrow and milestone release.
- Grant distribution fee for NGOs, DAOs, hackathons, and sponsors.
- Verification or document review fee.
- Premium compliance and reporting exports.
- Premium creator tools for analytics, campaign optimization, and donor CRM.
- Payment partner economics where legally and commercially appropriate.

Pricing logic:
- Standard campaign: low-friction creator acquisition.
- Protected campaign: higher-value trust workflow.
- Program account: workflow pricing based on grantees, milestones, or payout volume.

Important note:
Only the `0%` first campaign and `5%` later campaign fee are currently stated in the product. Treat the Protect pricing as proposed strategy until finalized.

Suggested visual:
Revenue stack:
Campaign fees -> Protect workflow -> Program tooling -> Premium tools.

Speaker note:
This is where Protect improves the business model. It gives OneRaise a reason to earn more than a thin donation fee.

## 60-Second Pitch Script

OneRaise is a borderless crowdfunding platform with trust built into the money movement.

Today, creators and changemakers can raise online, but backers often lose visibility once funds leave their account. That trust gap is even worse for cross-border giving, emergency aid, and grants, where donors need confidence and recipients need payment rails that actually work in their region.

OneRaise solves this with global campaign pages, multi-rail payments, stablecoin settlement, and OneRaise Protect. Backers can donate by card, local transfer, crypto, or Solana-routed tokens. For protected campaigns, funds can sit in escrow and unlock only when milestones, documents, receipts, proof-of-work, or verifier approvals prove progress.

That means creators get access to global capital, backers get certainty of impact, and NGOs, DAOs, and grant programs get a better way to distribute funds with accountability.

Our wedge is high-trust funding: emergency aid, community projects, creator campaigns, and milestone-based grants. OneRaise helps money move globally. Protect makes sure it moves with proof.

## 30-Second Pitch Script

OneRaise is accountable crowdfunding for a borderless world. It helps creators, communities, NGOs, and grant programs raise globally while OneRaise Protect adds escrow, verification, milestone releases, refunds, and transparent records. Donors can give through familiar payment methods or crypto rails, while creators and grantees unlock funds by showing progress. The result is a funding platform where money does not just move faster. It moves with proof.

## Slide Design Rules

- Use product UI more than generic photography.
- Make Protect visible in the middle of the deck, not only as a late feature.
- Show balances as separated buckets: Total Protected, Refundable, Pending Review, Released.
- Use status language consistently: Funding, Locked, Pending Verification, Released, Refunded.
- Use green for verified, approved, released, and successful states.
- Use gold for pending review, urgency, and risk.
- Use blue for refundable funds and backer protection.
- Use maps, ledgers, campaign pages, escrow consoles, payment rails, receipts, and proof artifacts.
- Avoid generic crypto visuals. OneRaise is about accountability, not speculation.
- Repeat "trust", "proof", "escrow", "milestone", and "release" across the deck.
