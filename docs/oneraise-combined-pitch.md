# OneRaise + OneRaise Protect Combined Pitch

## Core Framing

Do not pitch OneRaise and OneRaise Protect as two separate products.

Pitch them as one system:

**OneRaise is the platform. OneRaise Protect is the trust layer.**

Short version:

**OneRaise moves funds across borders. OneRaise Protect makes sure those funds move with proof.**

## 30-Second Pitch

OneRaise is accountable crowdfunding for a borderless world. It helps creators, communities, NGOs, and grant programs raise money globally through campaign pages, multi-rail payments, and tracked donations. But crowdfunding has a trust problem: donors often do not know what happens after they give. That is why we built OneRaise Protect, the escrow and verification layer that holds funds, verifies milestones, releases money when progress is proven, and keeps refunds possible when campaigns fail. OneRaise helps people raise globally. Protect makes the money move responsibly.

## 60-Second Pitch

OneRaise is a borderless crowdfunding platform for creators, communities, NGOs, emergency aid teams, and grant programs.

Today, raising money online is easier than ever, but trust is still broken. Backers often lose visibility after they donate. Creators in emerging markets struggle with payment access. Emergency aid campaigns need speed, but donors still need proof. Grant programs need milestone payouts, not manual spreadsheets and blind transfers.

OneRaise solves the access problem with global campaign pages, multi-rail payments, donor records, creator dashboards, and payout options. Donors can support campaigns through familiar payment methods, local rails, crypto deposits, or Solana-routed tokens.

Then OneRaise Protect solves the trust problem. For high-trust campaigns, funds can sit in escrow. Creators submit proof such as receipts, photos, documents, reports, vendor details, or proof-of-work. Approved milestones unlock payouts. If a campaign fails its conditions, funds can remain refundable or be redirected according to the campaign rules.

So the combined vision is simple: OneRaise helps people raise globally, and OneRaise Protect makes sure funds move with proof.

## 2-Minute Pitch

OneRaise is building the trust layer for global crowdfunding.

The problem is that crowdfunding has focused too much on collecting money and not enough on proving what happens after the money moves. A donor may support a medical campaign, a community project, a creator, or a grant program, but once the payment is made, visibility often disappears. That creates fear for backers, friction for credible creators, and operational headaches for NGOs, DAOs, hackathons, and sponsors that need to release funds responsibly.

OneRaise starts with the core crowdfunding platform. A creator or organization can launch a campaign page, set a funding goal, tell their story, receive donations, track backers, and manage payout options. The platform is built for borderless fundraising, so supporters are not forced into one payment rail. They can give through card, local transfer, crypto deposit, or Solana-routed token flows, while OneRaise records the campaign, donor, provider, asset, network, and payment status.

But the real wedge is OneRaise Protect.

Protect adds escrow, verification, milestone releases, refunds, and transparent payout records. For campaigns where trust matters, funds do not need to move upfront. They can be locked until campaign rules are satisfied. Creators or grantees submit proof such as documents, receipts, photos, reports, vendor details, or proof-of-work. A verifier or admin reviews the evidence. Once approved, the next tranche is released to the creator, vendor, or grantee. If milestones are not met, funds can remain refundable or be redirected according to the campaign rules.

This makes OneRaise useful across several high-value use cases: normal creator campaigns, protected crowdfunding, emergency aid escrow, medical fundraising, community infrastructure, and grant distribution for NGOs, DAOs, hackathons, and accelerators.

The simple pitch is this: OneRaise gives people access to global funding. OneRaise Protect gives backers confidence that money will only move when progress is proven.

## Storytelling Hooks

Use one of these at the beginning of a pitch or demo video. Keep the tone human and specific. The goal is to make the trust problem feel real before explaining the product.

### Story 1: The Donor Who Wanted To Help, But Needed Proof

Imagine a woman named Amara.

She is scrolling through her phone after work when she sees a campaign for a child who needs urgent surgery. The story feels real. The photos are painful. The amount needed is not impossible, so she wants to help.

But then she pauses.

She has donated before. She remembers sending money to a campaign and never hearing anything again. No receipt beyond the payment confirmation. No update. No proof that the person received help. No way to know if the money actually reached the hospital.

So this time, even though she cares, she hesitates.

That hesitation is the problem OneRaise is solving.

With OneRaise Protect, Amara can see that the campaign is protected. Her donation is tracked. The funds can sit in escrow. The campaign owner must submit hospital documents, receipts, or verifier-approved proof before the next payout is released. If the campaign fails its conditions, the money can remain refundable according to the campaign rules.

Now Amara is not being asked to trust blindly.

She is being shown the path from donation to proof to release.

That is the difference between giving with hope and giving with confidence.

### Story 2: The Creator Who Was Credible, But Could Not Prove It

Imagine a young builder named Tunde.

He wants to raise money for a solar-powered charging station in his community. The need is real. The plan is practical. He has already spoken to a local installer, priced the equipment, and found a location.

But when he shares the campaign, people ask the same questions:

"How do we know the money will be used properly?"

"What happens if the project stops halfway?"

"Can we see proof before all the funds are released?"

Tunde is not trying to scam anyone. He simply does not have a system that helps him prove trust step by step.

That is where OneRaise Protect changes the story.

Instead of asking backers to release all the money upfront, Tunde breaks the campaign into milestones. First, raise enough for the deposit. Then upload the vendor receipt. Then unlock the next tranche for equipment. Then submit photos and installation proof before the final payout.

Backers do not just see a fundraising goal.

They see progress.

Tunde does not just receive donations.

He earns trust as he builds.

OneRaise Protect helps credible creators prove they are credible.
